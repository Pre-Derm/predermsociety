SKIN SCIENCE · LESSON 03

MOISTURIZING

A simple introduction to moisturizers and how they support the skin barrier and hydration.

01 · THE FOUNDATION

Hydration
matters.

The skin barrier helps limit water loss from the body and helps protect the skin from the outside environment.

Moisturizers can support this process by adding water to the outer layers of skin, helping reduce water loss, and improving the feel and flexibility of the skin.

02 · MOISTURIZING SCIENCE

What does
moisturizer do?

Moisturizers are products designed to improve skin hydration and support the skin barrier. Different ingredients can accomplish this in different ways.

THE BIG IDEA

Hydrated skin
isn't oily skin.

Skin hydration refers to the amount of water present in the outer layers of the skin. Oiliness refers mainly to the amount of sebum produced. They are different concepts.

This means a person can have oily skin that still needs hydration, just as someone with dry skin may benefit from a moisturizer that supports the skin barrier.

03 · UNDERSTANDING INGREDIENTS

Three ways
moisturizers work.

Many moisturizers contain combinations of ingredients that help hydrate and protect the outer layer of skin.

01

Humectants

Humectants help attract and hold water in the outer layers of the skin. Examples include glycerin and hyaluronic acid.

02

Emollients

Emollients help smooth the spaces between skin cells and can make the skin feel softer and smoother.

03

Occlusives

Occlusives help reduce water loss by forming a protective layer over the skin. Petrolatum is one example.

04 · KEY IDEA

Moisturizing
is about balance.

A moisturizer does not need to feel heavy or greasy to be useful. The goal is to choose a formula that supports the skin's needs and feels comfortable enough to use consistently.
