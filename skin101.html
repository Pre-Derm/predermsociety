Skin Science · Lesson 01

Skin
101.

Before understanding dermatology, we have to understand the organ at the center of it all: the skin.

Your skin is
an organ.

The skin is the body's largest organ. It helps protect us from the outside environment, helps regulate body temperature, and allows us to sense what is happening around us.

Why does skin matter?

Healthy skin plays an important role in protection, sensation, temperature regulation, and maintaining the body's internal balance.

The three
main layers.

Skin has three major layers: the epidermis, dermis, and subcutaneous layer. Each layer has different structures and functions.

Epidermis

The outermost layer. It forms much of the skin's protective barrier.

Dermis

The middle layer. It contains structures such as blood vessels, nerves, glands, and hair follicles.

Subcutaneous layer

The deeper layer beneath the dermis. It contains connective tissue and fat and helps cushion and insulate the body.

The skin
barrier.

One of the skin's most important jobs is acting as a barrier. The outer layers help limit water loss and protect the body from many outside environmental factors.
