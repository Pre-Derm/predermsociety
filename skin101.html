Skin 101.

An introduction to the science of skin — what it is, how it works, and why understanding it matters.

What exactly is skin?

Skin is the body's largest organ. It creates a protective boundary between your body and the world around you.

It protects the body, helps regulate temperature, allows us to sense our surroundings, and plays an important role in maintaining the body's balance.

Your skin is always changing.

New skin cells are continually produced in deeper parts of the epidermis and gradually move toward the surface.

Beneath the surface.

Skin is organized into three major layers. Each layer has a different role.

Epidermis

The outermost layer of skin. It forms a protective barrier and contains cells called keratinocytes.

Dermis

The middle layer. It contains blood vessels, nerves, hair follicles, and glands.

Hypodermis

The deepest layer. It contains connective tissue and helps cushion and insulate the body.

Why does skin health matter?

Skin health is about more than appearance. Your skin is one of the body's first lines of defense.

Understanding basic skin science can help you better understand skincare information, common skin concerns, and dermatology.
