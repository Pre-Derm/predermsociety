SKIN JOURNEY · LESSON 02

SKIN
BARRIER

Explore the skin barrier, why it matters, and how the outer layers of skin help protect the body every day.

01 · THE FOUNDATION

Your skin
has a job.

The skin is more than an outer covering. One of its most important functions is creating a barrier between your body and the outside environment.

This barrier helps protect the body while also helping the skin maintain the right amount of water and supporting its normal functions.

02 · SKIN SCIENCE

Think of it
like a wall.

A simple way to understand the outer skin barrier is to imagine a brick wall. The cells and lipids in the outermost part of the epidermis work together to create a protective structure.

THE BIG IDEA

Protection + balance.

The skin barrier helps separate the inside of the body from the outside world. It also plays an important role in limiting water loss from the skin.

Understanding the barrier gives us a foundation for understanding many areas of dermatology.

03 · THE BRICK WALL

Cells + lipids.
Working together.

The outermost part of the epidermis can be compared to a brick wall. Specialized cells called corneocytes act like the bricks, while lipids between them help form the surrounding structure.

THE ANALOGY

Corneocytes are like the bricks. Lipids between the cells help form the surrounding matrix — similar to mortar in a wall.

04 · WHAT DOES IT DO?

The barrier
has several jobs.

01

Protection

The skin helps protect the body from environmental factors and forms an important part of the body's defenses.

02

Water

The outer skin barrier helps reduce the amount of water that escapes from the skin.

03

Balance

A functioning skin barrier helps support the skin's normal environment and biological functions.

05 · MEET THE STRATUM CORNEUM

The outermost
layer of the epidermis.

The stratum corneum is the outermost layer of the epidermis. It contains many layers of flattened, dead skin cells surrounded by lipids.

REMEMBER THIS

Stratum corneum = the outermost layer of the epidermis and an important part of the skin barrier.
