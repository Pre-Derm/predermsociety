STARTER

A beginner-friendly guide to understanding medical research, asking better questions, and exploring scientific literature.

01 · THE JOURNEY

From curiosity
to research.

Research begins with a question. This guide breaks the process into manageable steps for students who are curious about medicine and science.

01 · ASK

Find a question.

Start with something you genuinely wonder about. A strong research question is specific enough to investigate and meaningful enough to explore.

02 · EXPLORE

Read the literature.

Look at what researchers already know. Compare multiple reliable scientific sources rather than relying on a single webpage.

03 · PLAN

Build your approach.

Think about what information or data would help answer your question and what methods would be appropriate.

04 · EVALUATE

Check your sources.

Consider who published the information, what evidence supports it, when it was published, and whether the source is appropriate for your question.

05 · UNDERSTAND

Read a paper.

Learn to recognize the abstract, introduction, methods, results, and discussion sections of a scientific paper.

06 · COMMUNICATE

Share what you learn.

Research becomes more useful when findings can be communicated clearly through posters, presentations, papers, or educational projects.

02 · RESEARCH ETHICS

Good research
starts responsibly.

Research involving people, personal information, animals, or biological samples can involve important ethical and safety requirements. Students should work with qualified adults and follow the rules of their school, institution, or research program.

REMEMBER

Never collect personal or medical information from people for a project without appropriate supervision and approval.
